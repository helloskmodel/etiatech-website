**One-sentence answer:** Spot curing focuses light through a light guide or LED head into a millimeter-scale spot with very high peak irradiance (37 W/cm² mercury, 27.3 W/cm² LED) for precision adhesive points; area curing uses an emitting window from tens of millimeters to over a meter to cover coatings, inks or laminated surfaces at 2–25 W/cm², with modules that tile together. The criterion is "how large an area, and how many places".

## Comparison

| Item | Spot curing | Area curing |
|---|---|---|
| Spot / window | 3–12 mm spot | 76 × 10 mm to 1,350 × 20 mm |
| Peak irradiance | Very high | Moderate |
| Multi-point | Multi-leg light guides, cure rings, multi-channel controllers | Modules tiled end to end |
| Typical processes | Lens coupling, catheter bonding, component fixing, MEMS packaging | Coating, printing, display lamination, conformal coating, potting |
| Automation | Mounted on dispensing/alignment equipment | Mounted on conveyors/presses |

## Light delivery for spot curing

- Liquid light guide: single point, economical.
- High-power fiber light guide: 2–4 legs with equal output per leg, one system cures several points.
- Cure ring: couples to a light guide for 360° illumination of cylindrical parts such as catheters.
- LED head with interchangeable lens: 3 mm for peak irradiance, 6–12 mm for larger spots, 5 mm cylindrical lens for a line.

## FAQ

**Q: Four adhesive dots: spot or area?**

A: If the dots fit within one window, one area source works; if each needs a different dose or timing, use 4-channel spot curing.

**Q: Can area curing handle cylindrical parts?**

A: Multi-side placement or part rotation works; small-diameter cylinders (catheters, fibers) are more uniform with a cure ring or fiber reflector unit.
