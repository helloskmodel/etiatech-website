**One-sentence answer:** A mercury lamp is a 200–600 nm broad-spectrum source compatible with almost any formulation, provides UVC and the highest peak irradiance, but needs warm-up, emits infrared heat and ozone, and lasts a few thousand hours. A UV LED is a 365–405 nm single-wavelength source with low energy use, tens of thousands of hours of life, instant on/off, no infrared heat and no ozone, but requires an LED formulation. The first criterion is the formulation spectrum; the second is substrate heat sensitivity and cycle time.

## Comparison

| Item | Mercury lamp (short arc / medium pressure) | UV LED |
|---|---|---|
| Spectrum | 200–600 nm broad, filterable | Single peak ±10 nm |
| UVC | Yes | No |
| Peak irradiance | Up to 37 W/cm² for spot curing | 27.3 W/cm² spot, 20 W/cm² area |
| Lifetime | Short arc guaranteed 2,000 h (typical 3,500–4,000 h); medium pressure ≈1,000 h | Tens of thousands of hours; production lines exceed 50,000 h |
| Switching | ~4 min warm-up, shutter needed | Milliseconds, can be pulsed |
| Infrared heat | Significant | None |
| Ozone / mercury | Yes / contains mercury | No / no mercury |
| Power adjustment | Limited | 5–100% in 1% steps |
| Energy | Baseline | 50%+ lower |
| Consumables | Bulb, shutter, reflector | None |
| Formulation | Almost any | LED-specific |

## When to choose a mercury lamp

UVC surface cure is required; a validated legacy formulation should not be re-qualified; the highest peak irradiance is needed; the formulation is undecided in development and one machine must cover the full band.

## When to choose LED

The formulation is a 365–405 nm system; the substrate is heat-sensitive; short cycle times with frequent on/off; multiple independently controlled points; lower energy, exhaust and consumable costs; mercury-free requirements.

## Mixed use

Many lines keep both: LED for primary cure and mercury for a UVC post-cure, or LED for new products while legacy products stay on mercury.

## FAQ

**Q: Does switching from mercury to LED require process re-validation?**

A: The spectrum changes, the formulation usually changes, so yes.

**Q: What does the 2,000-hour mercury lamp life mean?**

A: Excelitas' guaranteed life for S-series short-arc bulbs; typical use is 3,500–4,000 hours.
